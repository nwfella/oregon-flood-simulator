How this is computed & what it is not

Source. A Cascadia megathrust rupture whose length scales as L = 50·100.65(M−7) km and whose slip follows from the seismic moment (M₀ = 101.5M+9.1 N·m, μ = 35 GPa, 110 km downdip width). Initial sea-surface displacement is taken as 70% of slip.

Propagation. Huygens/Dijkstra front over the real bathymetry with local speed √(g·h), emitted progressively along the rupture at 2.5 km/s. Amplitude evolves by Green's law (h−1/4) plus a path-length attenuation. Travel times therefore fall out of the basin geometry rather than being hard-coded.

Inundation. Runup at the shoreline comes from the shoaled amplitude through a saturation curve (breaking limit), modulated by local bay openness and slope from the DEM, plus your tide setting and modelled coseismic subsidence (0.18 × slip, capped at 2.5 m). Flooding then spreads inland by a static "bathtub" fill with a flow-front timing from √(g·depth) — the standard regional evacuation-mapping approach.

Elevation. 612 × 900 grid (~520 m cells) resampled from AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM/NED on land, ETOPO bathymetry offshore). Populations are rounded 2020 census figures; "people in zone" is population × the flooded fraction of each town's 2.5 km disc — an exposure indicator, not a casualty forecast.

Arrival times. With the animation running, a 520 m grid puts the front crossing the 30 km of 50–150 m deep shelf at 10–30 m/s, so the model is a few minutes slower than the DOGAMI/NOAA figures: a weak leading signal reaches the beach in the first 5–10 minutes (that is the coseismic withdrawal you would see), the main wave arrives around 20 minutes, and water is pushing into town at 30+. Real guidance of "15–20 minutes" refers to the main wave at the beach.

Resolution. 520 m cells cannot resolve a 300 m beach berm. Coastal strips narrower than a cell are averaged into the terrain around them, so inundation is under-reported where the coast is steep — most of all on the south coast, where the model shows far less flooding than the official maps do. Estuaries and wide flats (Clatsop plain, Tillamook, the Columbia) are the parts it resolves best.

What it is not. Not a hydrodynamic model (no momentum, no wave breaking, no sediment, no vertical evacuation). Runup in a real CSZ M9 is estimated at 9–30 m across source scenarios, with ~10 m the preferred value (Witter et al./DOGAMI Cannon Beach study). Treat this as a physically-scaled visualisation of hazard geometry, and use the official DOGAMI/NTHMP maps and local evacuation routes for decisions.
